Fire Safety
Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005
Plain-English summary for Scratch Golf operations. This is guidance, not legal advice. Last reviewed October 2026. Always check the wording on legislation.gov.uk, and confirm frequencies and standards for each venue with your competent fire risk assessor.
Your venues (as told to us, October 2026)
  • Responsible person: Tom is overall responsible for fire safety. Day-to-day tasks can be done by other team members, but the duty stays with the responsible person, so keep a record of who has been given which tasks. Upload it under Documents as a fire safety arrangements document.
  • Balham door: magnetic lock. It should release when the fire alarm operates and if power is lost, with a signed emergency door release unit beside the exit. The weekly alarm test checks the alarm release, and Upcoming requirements has a fuller test.
  • Wandsworth door: Codelocks keypad lock. The key point is that anyone inside can always open the door straight away without the code or a key. Ask your assessor whether the lock should also be linked to the alarm. The weekly check confirms free exit and that the keypad and battery are working.
  • Leased units: both units are leased. Scratch Golf is a responsible person for the units it occupies, and the landlord, freeholder or managing agent is usually responsible for the common and shared parts. Where there is more than one responsible person you must co-operate and co-ordinate. See "Leased units: what to agree with your landlord" below.
  • Still to confirm: whether the fire alarm and emergency lighting are your own systems or part of a building-wide system run by the landlord (and who services them), whether a current fire risk assessment exists and who carried it out, and whether the alarm is remotely monitored.
Who this applies to
  • The Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 (the Fire Safety Order) is the main fire safety law for non-domestic premises in England and Wales, including commercial premises the public can use. Both venues are in London, so it applies to both.
  • The duties sit with the responsible person: the employer, or whoever has control of the premises (owner, landlord, occupier, managing agent). If there is more than one responsible person, for example a landlord who controls the common parts, you must work together and co-ordinate.
  • Local fire and rescue authorities inspect premises and can serve notices. Failing to comply can lead to fines or imprisonment.

Leased units: what to agree with your landlord
  • GOV.UK says that in shared premises there is likely to be more than one responsible person, and that they must co-ordinate their fire safety plans. For common or shared areas the responsible person is the landlord, freeholder or managing agent.
  • Since 1 October 2023 each responsible person must take reasonable steps to find out who the other responsible persons are and make themselves known to them, and must pass relevant fire safety information to an incoming responsible person.
  • Ask the landlord or managing agent for: the fire risk assessment for the building and common parts; details of any alarm, emergency lighting or other fire systems that serve your unit but belong to them; the service and test certificates for those systems; the building evacuation strategy and assembly point; and a named contact for fire safety.
  • Give them yours: your fire risk assessment for the unit, your emergency contact, and any change that affects the building, such as new locks, new fit-out or a change in how the unit is used.
  • Check your lease. Commercial leases commonly require the tenant to comply with all statutory requirements, to keep systems inside the unit maintained, and to get the landlord's consent before altering the unit. They also often say who maintains shared systems and how that is charged through the service charge. Your solicitor can confirm what yours says, including the keypad and magnetic lock fitted at the entrances.
  • Store what you receive under Documents using the "Landlord / lease fire information" type. The annual landlord co-ordination check is on the Upcoming requirements tab.

What every record should show
  • The date and the venue.
  • What was checked or tested, specific enough to repeat (for example which call point).
  • The result: satisfactory, defect, or not applicable with a reason. Nothing left blank.
  • Who did it, by name. For contractors, the company and engineer, with the certificate.
  • Any defect, what was done about it, and when it was fixed. Defects raised here create an item in Actions.
  • Evidence where relevant, such as a certificate or photo.
  • Records are never overwritten or deleted. A mistake is corrected by adding a new entry that refers to the old one, so the original stays on file.
  • Keep records so they can be shown to a fire inspector on request. Ask your assessor and insurer for a minimum retention period and do not delete anything before then.
Points to confirm with your fire risk assessor (unstaffed venues)
These are discussion points, not statements of what the law requires. They are the areas an unstaffed, access-controlled venue most often needs the assessment to cover explicitly.
  • How people in a bay are warned of a fire, and how they get out, when nobody from the business is on site.
  • Whether the alarm is remotely monitored, who is called, and who responds.
  • That the Balham magnetic lock releases on alarm and on power failure, and that the Wandsworth Codelocks door can always be opened from inside without the code. Fire authority logbook guidance is to test automatic door releases weekly with the alarm test.
  • How members are told what to do: fire action notices, booking confirmations, onboarding.
  • Who does the weekly tests, and how they are briefed. Cleaners currently record a weekly alarm test on the cleaning form.
  • Heat and ignition risks specific to simulator bays: projectors, PCs, chargers, ventilation, cabling, stored packaging.
  • Lone or out-of-hours use, and any lease or insurance conditions that add requirements.
